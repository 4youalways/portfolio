# Abstract
## Background:
Klebsiella pneumoniae is a significant contributor to multidrug-resistant healthcare-associated infections and is particularly prevalent among neonatal bloodstream infections at Queen Elizabeth Central Hospital (QECH), Blantyre Malawi. K. pneumoniae belonging to sequence type (ST)39 caused 135 cases between 2005 and 2020. Seventy-four percent of these (n = 100/135) were isolated in 2017. This study aims to further investigate these ST39 isolates to identify whether any key evolutionary steps may have led to the expansion of the lineage in 2017.

## Methods:
I analyzed 135 whole genome sequences of K. pneumoniae  ST39 isolates collected at QECH, comparing 2017 isolates to those collected before and after. I created high-quality hybrid genome assemblies of representative strains from this collection and performed AMR gene and plasmid screening, phylogenetic, and pangenome analyses.

## Results:
Bioinformatic analyses exploring the antimicrobial resistance, bacteriophage, plasmid, and remaining pangenome content of ST39 isolated at QECH identified three regions within the genomes of ST39 K. pneumoniae that gained additional coding sequences through prophage integrases and insertion sequences during the 2017 outbreak. These coding sequences may have contributed to the expansion of the clone in 2017 as they are absent in isolates collected before and after this period. 

## Conclusions:
This study highlighted regions of the ST39 genome which may be used as potential markers for strains that are better adapted to the hospital environment leading to healthcare-associated infections. These chromosomal changes need further investigation to help guide infection prevention and control practices.
